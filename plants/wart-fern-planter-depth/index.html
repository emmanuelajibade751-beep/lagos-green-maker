LAGOS GREEN MAKER   /   WART FERN · LAG-320

How shallow can the planter be?

In this proposed test, two wart ferns start with the same room to spread sideways. One has less soil beneath its roots.

Both drawings show planting day at the same scale. The soil starts at the same height. Only the base moves.

Do both keep making healthy new fronds?

How often does each need water?

How we’d test it
  1. Measure the starting rootballs. Choose a shallow depth that fits the roots without bending or trimming them. Use a deeper planter for comparison. With the same width and length, the deeper planter also holds more soil. Record usable soil depth and volume for each. Keep the inside width and length, growing mix, packing and drainage holes the same.
  2. Use several separate planters of each depth. Pair similar fern divisions by rootball size and frond count, then randomly assign one of each pair to each depth. Match light and exposure across positions.
  3. Check moisture at the same depth within the starting root zone. Water each planter when it reaches the same dry threshold and bring it back to the same moisture target. Record when and how much water you add and how much drains out. This tells us how often each depth needs care under this watering rule.
  4. Over the same growing period, count surviving ferns and healthy new fronds. At the end, look for roots packed along the base or circling the walls. Compare each pair and the variation across planters. If the deeper reference also struggles, the trial cannot establish a healthy fit.

These two depths test whether the shallow candidate works. Finding the shallowest workable depth would take further trials.

Research behind this

Singapore’s National Parks Board lists well-drained soil for wart fern (Microsorum scolopendria). Its record gives no minimum planter depth. Both the earlier water-level idea and this depth test still need real plants. Smaller roots alone cannot tell us whether a shallow holder will keep a fern healthy.
